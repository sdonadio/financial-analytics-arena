WEEK 9

Market Microstructure & Execution Analytics

You stand up a real market, trade into it, and then measure the same 200-share order two ways. TCA against the prevailing mid says your execution cost −2.49 bps. Implementation shortfall against the price you decided at says −24.62 bps. Both are computed correctly, and the ten-times gap is the whole lesson: a benchmark you move is a benchmark that flatters you. Then three surveillance rules that catch a wash pair perfectly on a live tape accuse two innocent bots on a clean one.

  • Read a limit order book properly: spread, depth, touch imbalance, microprice, and what price-time priority does to a passive order you just posted.
  • Split a shortfall into delay, impact-plus-spread and opportunity cost — and say which desk owns each term.
  • Read the maker column of a TCA report and explain why retail flow paid 6.07 bps while the market maker was paid 4.51.
  • Run three surveillance detectors, name the abuser all three miss, and explain why a measured precision of 1.00 is not a precision.
  • Distinguish a quoted spread from an effective one from a trade-weighted one, and know what stale_pct = 95% means for a consumer that assumes a message is news.

Part 1

1What is it? The price is not a number, it is a queue

Every price you have used for nine weeks was a summary of a queue: a list of resting limit orders on both sides, sorted by price and then by arrival time. Microstructure is the study of that queue, and execution analytics is the business of measuring what it cost you to get through it.

Four numbers off one snapshot

  • Spread — best ask minus best bid, always quoted in bps of the mid, because a 30-cent spread is 6 bps on MSFT and 27 bps on INTC.
  • Depth — size at the touch, and cumulative size over five levels. The first number decides whether your child order fills; the second decides what your parent costs.
  • Imbalance — (bid_sz − ask_sz)/(bid_sz + ask_sz), the only free directional information in a book.
  • Microprice — the size-weighted mid, which sits off the arithmetic mid by roughly imbalance × half-spread.

Price-time priority

  • An incoming order scans the opposite side from the best price inward.
  • If prices cross, the trade executes at the resting order’s price — the aggressor pays the spread, the passive side earns it.
  • At the same price, earlier orders fill first. Post at the touch behind 40 shares and 40 shares must trade before you do.
  • A market order never rests; the remainder is cancelled. That is why a slicing algorithm exists at all.

The lab’s measured run. Buy 200 AAPL, filled 146 in 10.0 s. Decision mid 309.5150, first child mid 309.4350, final mid 310.7650, execution VWAP 310.0967. Delay +11.68 ($, +1.89 bps, and it is luck — the mid drifted 8 cents our way between deciding and sending). Impact plus spread −96.61 (−15.61 bps). Shortfall −84.93. Opportunity cost on the 54 shares we never got, marked at the final mid, −67.50 (−10.90 bps) — over half the total. Total −152.43, or −24.62 bps.

Push the slice count up and the interval out: the impact term falls and the opportunity term rises, because a slower schedule leaks less information and misses more of the move. Finding that crossover is the entire content of execution-algorithm design — and switching from crossing to posting trades one cost for another, with a fill rate you must report rather than average away.

Part 3

3Why it matters: measuring the market, and policing it

Two things stand between a tape and a defensible liquidity section. The first is knowing which spread you quoted. The second is knowing whether the volume you measured was real.

Widget 4 · quoted, effective, and trade-weighted spread

The lab’s broker quotes a fixed 30 cents on every name, which is 6.4 bps on MSFT at $485 and 26.6 bps on INTC near $90. A spread in cents is not a spread. And the effective spread — what takers actually paid, 7.44 bps on AAPL — sits below the quoted 10.02 bps, which is not a discount but a weighting: trades cluster when the spread is tight.

quoted, time-weighted effective / trade-weighted

On AAPL the time-weighted median quoted spread was 10.021 bps over 444 snapshots and the trade-weighted median was 7.444 bps over 93 prints — to three decimals the effective spread, so every AAPL trade happened at the touch and the entire gap is clustering. A time-weighted spread describes the screen; a trade-weighted one describes what was paid, and quoting one while calling it the other is the commonest error in a liquidity section.

Liveness is not “the data is arriving”. In the lab stale_pct was 92–97% on every symbol except the one the bots were fighting over, where it was 59.7%. An event-driven feed goes quiet, and a consumer written to assume “a message means news” will mistake a dead market for a calm one. Separately: 32 of AAPL’s 93 prints were the wash pair, so a third of its volume transferred no risk. Never use share volume as a liquidity proxy on a tape you have not surveilled.

Three findings, in increasing order of importance. The stuffer is unmissable: 2,565 orders, zero fills, a peak of 68 messages a second and 70 distinct seconds over the venue’s quota. The wash pair is textbook: reciprocity 0.938, 93% of each account’s volume done with the other, net/gross 0.091. But the honest market maker and the honest retail flow score reciprocity 0.868 and net/gross 0.258 — two of the rule’s three features — and are acquitted only by concentration, 0.336 against a 0.50 threshold. In a market with one liquidity provider, everyone’s concentration is high, so the thinnest discriminator is the one carrying the whole rule.
And the genuine failure. rogue_1, the layerer, is flagged by none of the three: it fills often enough (40 times) that its cancel-to-fill ratio is only 5.8, its message rate never trips the quota, and it never trades with itself. Lowering the threshold catches it and tells you nothing about your precision, because the only firms in this audit trail are the four guilty ones — nobody else was asked to file. A precision measured on a dataset containing only suspects is not a precision. Layering needs the per-team resting size at each price level, which is sent only to observer connections and is never recorded.

Live session, or offline tape

Everything in this week except standing up the venue runs offline, and Phase 2 must not depend on the wifi. The offline recorder writes the same envelope the live server writes, so tca_report.py, the microstructure statistics and the self-match rule work unchanged; the two audit-trail rules need a local exchange and the rogue bots, which is still no internet. The only thing a recorded tape cannot give you is the live scored session — and that is a Phase-2 deliverable, not an analytics one.

Keep your own order log. The recording is broadcast-only, so your cancels, your rejects and your decision prices exist nowhere else, and neither the shortfall nor the cancel-to-fill number can be recovered afterwards.

The venue proving its own books balance: every share bought was sold, no position breaks, no cash gap. An operations analyst reads that line before any P&L, and so should you — a tape whose recon failed is a tape whose numbers you cannot cite. Note too that the broker in this session was quoting off the exchange’s own mark, because Yahoo was unreachable: correct engineering, but a market whose only price reference is itself has no outside anchor, and that is worth one sentence in any report written off the tape.

Part 4

4Five ways this goes wrong

Each of these produced a number that looked fine and meant something else. Open them.

Benchmarking your fills against a mid you moved

TCA measures every fill against the mid prevailing at that moment. By the fourth child of our parent order that mid was already 30 cents higher, because we had bought the first three. So the report says −2.49 bps while the shortfall against the decision price says −24.62 bps — a ten-times difference, and neither number is computed incorrectly.

The same trap in miniature: judging a child order against the arrival price of that child rather than of the parent. Every slice looks excellent and the parent still cost 25 bps.

Fix: report both, and if you report only one, report the shortfall. Record the decision mid at the instant the decision is made — no tape contains it — and mark the unfilled remainder at the final mid rather than dropping it.

Quoting a spread in cents, or a time-weighted spread as though takers paid it

A fixed 30-cent quote is 6.4 bps on MSFT and 26.6 bps on INTC. Any cross-sectional statement about liquidity built on cents is a statement about price levels. And on AAPL the time-weighted median quoted spread was 10.021 bps while the trade-weighted median was 7.444 — because trades cluster when the spread is tight, not because anyone got a discount.

Then the volume trap: 32 of AAPL’s 93 prints were a wash pair, so a third of the “volume” moved no risk at all.

Fix: always bps of the contemporaneous mid; state whether a spread is time-weighted, trade-weighted or effective; and compute the realised spread too — the mid five seconds after the trade against the trade price — because effective minus realised is adverse selection, the part of the spread the maker does not keep.

Reading a healthy socket as a healthy market

The most dangerous state in this platform: 547 messages arrive in twelve seconds, ten of eleven books are two-sided, best_bid and best_ask are populated, the spreads are sane — and the market has been dead for thirty-seven seconds, because a disconnected participant’s resting orders are never cancelled. Your bot keeps trading against a corpse, and its fills are real.

Its analytic cousin is stale_pct = 95%: nine snapshots in ten were bit-identical to the one before. Any statistic averaged over snapshots is then averaging over a book that is not changing, which silently reweights everything towards the quiet symbols.

Fix: liveness means the data is changing. Compare two snapshots and assert that something moved; report stale_pct beside every snapshot-averaged statistic; and weight by trade time when you want to describe what was paid.

A surveillance rule shipped without a clean control

Run the self-match rule on a tape with no abuse in it at all and it fires: an honest mean-reversion bot trades both ways by construction (reciprocity 0.992), ends flat by construction (net/gross 0.017), and does 71% of its volume with the only market maker in the room. Every feature the rule keys on is a property of legitimate mean reversion against a monopoly liquidity provider.

The fix every real surveillance system uses is a known-entity list — and that list is the first thing an abuser asks to be added to. State your exemptions, and state who may change them.

Fix: never quote a detection rate without a false-positive rate measured on a session you know is clean, and never quote a precision computed on a population that contains only suspects. Publish the threshold sweep, not the threshold you liked.

Choosing the detector before checking what you are entitled to observe

Of the three rules, only self-match can be computed from a recording, because a recording is a broadcast log: no order acknowledgements, no cancels, no rejects, no per-team ladder. Cancel-to-fill and quote-stuffing need each participant’s own audit trail, which exists only because we asked the bots to write one. Layering needs ladder_snapshot — per-team resting size at each price level — which is sent to observer and teacher connections and never recorded at all.

So the abuser that all three miss is not a tuning failure. It is an evidence failure, and no threshold fixes it.

Fix: write down your evidence set before your rule set. For each rule, name the field it needs and the message type that carries it; if no recorded message carries it, either attach a collector or admit the rule is unimplementable on your data.

Part 6

6Do the work

In the lab (~75 min)

  • Bring up an exchange, a market maker and retail flow, then three abusive bots so the tape has something to find. Every client gets EXCHANGE_HOST=localhost — without it your “local” rogue attacks the hosted venue in front of the class.
  • Work a 200-share parent order through the book with a slicing algorithm that logs its own decision price, and decompose the shortfall.
  • Close the session, read the recon line, then run scripts/tca_report.py and put its −2.49 bps next to your −24.62.
  • Compute spread, depth, imbalance and stale_pct from book_snapshot alone, then the time- versus trade-weighted spread.
  • Three detectors, a threshold sweep, and the clean control that accuses the innocent.
