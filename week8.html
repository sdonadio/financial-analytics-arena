WEEK 8

Machine Learning for Finance

This week you fit a next-day-direction classifier, get an AUC of 0.5164 that would fund a desk, and then count the leak — 1,033,740 training rows sharing a date with a row in their own test fold. Rebuilt as purged, embargoed walk-forward the AUC lands on 0.5021, the coin toss, and the error bar gets 5.7× wider. Then the awkward part: a signal whose hit rate is below the base rate earns +29.56%/yr gross — and dies at 10 bps a side.

  • Build features and a label on the week-1 panel, and state the label horizon before you fit anything — it is the single number that decides how much of your training set is contaminated.
  • Run the same model under four split regimes and read the leak audit beside every AUC: same_time, label_overlap, embargoed.
  • Watch capacity move the leaky score up and the honest one down, and understand why that is a diagnosis rather than a hyperparameter problem.
  • Redo feature importance honestly — not one of eight features is positively distinguishable from zero, and the two that clear a standard deviation do so on the wrong side — then watch the leaky fit invent a confident explanation.
  • Translate AUC into money, find the cost level where the strategy dies, and leave with a model card whose last two rows separate an analyst from an enthusiast.

Part 1

1What is it? A supervised-learning problem with a clock in it

Machine learning for finance is ordinary supervised learning applied to data whose rows are not exchangeable. Two rows on the same date are not independent draws — the market moved and both moved with it — and a label at t is a function of a price at t+h, so a row can know something about its neighbour’s answer. Every technique in this week exists to handle those two facts.

Refuse anything below --min-coverage and record what the fallback cost you: CBOE is split-adjusted but not dividend-adjusted, which belongs on the model card, not in a footnote.

Eight trailing features

  • mom5, mom21, mom63 — trailing returns
  • rev1 — yesterday’s return
  • vol21 — 21-day realised vol
  • volume_z — volume, z-scored on 63 days
  • dist52 — distance below the 52-week high
  • xrank21 — cross-sectional rank of mom21

All computable at the close of day t, and all built with fill_method=None. That argument is not decoration: pct_change’s deprecated default pads NA values, carrying the last price across a gap and reporting a 0% return on a day with no price — fabricating returns on exactly the days that deserve suspicion.

Part 2

2How it works: four ways to split, and only one of them is honest

The same model, the same features, the same label — and an AUC that moves by 0.0144 depending on how the folds were cut. The point is not that leakage inflates a score. It is that leakage makes the folds agree, and false agreement reads as confidence.

The two knobs point opposite ways. Raising capacity from 50 iterations at depth 3 to 500 at depth 8 lifts the leaky score 0.5148 → 0.5182 and drops the honest one 0.5039 → 0.4997. Capacity is not buying alpha, it is buying a better memory for which day it is. If validation improves while out-of-sample degrades as you add capacity, you are not underfitting — you are measuring your own leak.

In a walk-forward the purge is a rounding error: even a 21-day label moves the AUC by −0.0010 — purging slightly helped, i.e. noise. The leak that mattered was the shuffle, three orders of magnitude bigger: 1,033,740 rows against 467. Keep the purge anyway, because it costs 0.002 AUC and it is your only defence once splits stop being one-sided. Just do not claim it saved you when the shuffle is what you fixed.

A leaky model reports a wrong score and a wrong explanation — and the explanation is the more persuasive of the two. Honestly measured, no feature is positively distinguishable from zero — six of eight sit inside one standard deviation of it and the other two are negative — the best helps in two of five folds, and four have negative mean importance. Part 3

3Why it matters: AUC 0.55 is a good day, until you pay for it

AUC is not a P&L, and the translation between them is where most ML-for-finance projects either become interesting or quietly stop. Score every out-of-sample row, sort each day’s cross-section into deciles, long the top, short the bottom, hold one day — and then subtract what it costs to do that 252 times a year.

Part 4

4Five ways this goes wrong

Every one of these has been submitted, defended, and only then diagnosed. Open them.

KFold(shuffle=True) on a panel — the million-row leak nobody sees

Shuffling puts AAPL-on-Tuesday in train and MSFT-on-Tuesday in test. They moved together because the market moved, so the model needs no signal at all — only the ability to recognise the day. Counted on the lab’s panel: 1,033,740 training rows share a date with a row in their own test fold, which is every training row, five times over. The AUC comes out at 0.5164 with five folds inside ±0.0019, and people trade 0.52.

The tell is not the mean, it is the agreement. Leakage lets every fold see the same days, so the folds concur, and false agreement reads as precision. The honest estimate on the same data is 5.7× less precise: you got to be confident and wrong at once.

Fix: split by date, never by row, and print a leak audit next to every score — same_time, label_overlap, embargoed. A number with a zeroed audit beside it is a result; a number alone is a claim.

Reaching for a bigger model when the honest AUC collapses

Everyone’s first instinct. Take the capacity from 50 iterations at depth 3 to 1,000 with unlimited depth and 255 leaves, and the two columns move in opposite directions: the leaky score climbs 0.5148 → 0.5182 while the purged one falls 0.5039 → 0.4997. The gap — the thing you are actually growing — widens from +0.0109 to +0.0183.

Capacity buys memory, and on a leaked split memory is rewarded. The diagnostic is general: if validation improves while out-of-sample degrades as you add capacity, the split is leaking. No amount of regularisation search fixes a fold structure.

Fix: fix the split first, then tune. Sweep capacity under the purged splitter only, and publish both columns of the sweep so a reader can see the gap rather than trust your conclusion.

Reading .feature_importances_ off a leaky fit and writing the economics paragraph

Impurity importance counts how often a column was split on, so it assigns importance to pure noise by construction — feed the model twelve random columns and it will rank them. Worse, the leaky fit hands you all eight real features with positive importance in a plausible order, and the plausible order is what gets written up.

Honestly measured on each walk-forward test block, the best feature (rev1, at 0.00154 ± 0.00664) helps in two of five folds, and four features have negative mean importance — the model would score better if we destroyed them. The correct sentence is: we have not established that any of these eight features carries next-day information.

Fix: permutation importance, per test block, with a standard deviation and a folds-positive count beside every value. Then include noise columns on purpose: if they rank, your ranking method is the finding.

Reporting AUC as though it were a strategy — or a P&L as though it were a signal

The two errors are mirror images and both appear in the same lab. A pooled AUC of 0.5016 with a top-decile hit rate below the base rate earns +29.56%/yr gross, because the model sorts by size of move rather than by probability of a positive sign. Quote the AUC alone and you have buried the result; quote the gross alone and you have buried the costs.

Then the ladder. At 1 bp a side it is +24.52%/yr; at 5 bps, +4.36% and a Sharpe of 0.08; at 10 bps — realistic all-in for a daily long-short in size — it loses 20.84% a year. A daily strategy turns the book over every day, so it pays the spread about 500 times a year.

Fix: report AUC, hit rate against the base rate, and the cost ladder in the same table, and name the cost level at which the strategy dies. Then ask whether a longer holding period is available to you, because that is the only lever that moves the cost term.

A result carried by two calendar years, sized as though it were nine

+11.73 bps/day on 2,238 days, with a standard deviation of 362.5, is a t-stat of 1.53 and a Sharpe of 0.514 ± 0.66 — an interval that contains zero. Then the calendar: 2018 gives +59.9 bps/day and 2025 gives +57.4, while the other eight years run between −11.5 and +3.8. Two years of ten carry the entire result, and they are the two volatility regimes — exactly when short-term reversal pays.

The exposure check confirms it: decile 9 holds yesterday’s losers (−76.1 bps), decile 0 yesterday’s winners (+116.7 bps), and both tails sit in the high-vol names. This is a bet on realised volatility wearing an ML costume, and it must be sized and described as one.

Fix: three refusals before you believe your own number — significance (t-stat and a Sharpe interval), exposure (regress on the factors you already know about, from week 2), and stability (the per-year bar chart). If two periods carry it, say so in the abstract, not the appendix.

6Do the work

In the lab (~75 min)

  • Rebuild the panel from a named source and read its sidecar — source, adjustment, as-of, coverage — then explain why a 62-of-100 pull is refused rather than used.
  • Build the eight features and the label, state the horizon, and point at the code that purges it.
  • Run all four split regimes with the leak audit printed beside each AUC, and explain the +0.0144 shuffle premium and the 5.7× wider honest error bar.
  • Sweep capacity and watch the two columns diverge; then measure the purge cost and be honest that it is a rounding error.
  • Permutation importance per test block, contrasted with the leaky ranking.
  • AUC → deciles → bps/day → %/yr → the cost ladder that kills it.

Homework 7 · purged-CV model card

  • Your own features, on your own universe. State the label and horizon before you fit anything.
  • A leak audit beside every AUC, and the honest-versus-leaky pair for your own splits.
  • Count your configurations out loud — including the ones you abandoned.
  • Grades attach to the model card and the falsification test, not the AUC: a well-measured 0.50 beats an unexplained 0.56.
