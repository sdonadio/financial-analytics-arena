WEEK 10

Review Day & Final

Three things happen in this room, in this order. The second live scored session, gated by a pre-flight that catches the failure where a market has been dead for thirty-seven seconds and 547 messages still arrive. Then peer analyst reviews — you review another team by re-running their numbers, not by reading their prose. Then the Phase-2 checklist and a structured review of the concept list the closed-book exam draws from.

  • Run a six-check go/no-go before you connect, and see it fail FRESH on a market that passed every other check.
  • Score a sample tearsheet against the published rubric, compare against the consensus, and find out how much of a review score is the reviewer.
  • Name the one number in your own tearsheet you would not sign — the row we grade hardest.
  • Close out Phase 2 for your track, with a readiness dial that goes green only when the reproducibility and honesty rows are done.
  • Walk the whole course back as one tearsheet, panel by panel, and mark every exam concept you cannot yet explain in two sentences.

Part 1

1What is it? A go/no-go, then a review, then an exam

Review day is not a wrap-up. It is three pieces of professional practice compressed into one session: a launch checklist, a peer review conducted by reproduction, and a closed-book examination of judgement rather than syntax.

Read the frozen case carefully, because it is the most dangerous state in this platform. The socket is up. 547 messages arrive in twelve seconds. Ten of eleven books are two-sided, best_bid and best_ask are populated, the spreads are sane. Every health check a student writes on the first attempt passes — and the market has been dead for thirty-seven seconds, because a disconnected participant’s resting orders are never cancelled. Your bot will keep trading against a corpse, and its fills will be real. Liveness is not “the data is arriving”, it is “the data is changing”, and the only way to know is to compare two snapshots.

Two rules that are not negotiable. Keep your own order log — the recording is broadcast-only, so cancels, rejects and decision prices exist nowhere else. And if the venue restarts mid-session, expect realized_pnl to read +0.00 for the rest of the run: that field is assigned by the exchange, whose ledger was genuinely reset. Your bot is right and the source of truth forgot. Reconcile to net worth, note the restart, and do not spend the session debugging your own counter.

Part 4

4Four ways review day goes wrong

Three of these cost teams the opening minutes of a scored session. The fourth costs them the grade. Open them.

Trading into a frozen book because the socket was healthy

547 messages in twelve seconds, ten of eleven books two-sided, spreads sane, session open — and nothing has changed for thirty-seven seconds because the market maker died and its resting orders were never cancelled. Your fills against that book are real, and so is the P&L.

The generalisation: every liveness check that counts arrivals rather than changes passes on a dead market. That includes “the websocket is open”, “we received a book_snapshot”, and “best_bid is not None”.

Fix: compare two snapshots and assert something moved, per symbol, and gate the launch on it — w10_preflight.py exits non-zero when anything WARNs or FAILs, so wire it into your launch script. Then add a seventh check: the age of your own last fill, so a bot that has stopped trading while the market moves flags itself.

Dialling the hosted arena from a “local” run

make register wrote the class arena’s address into .env, and shared/envfile.load_env() feeds it to every config module. So a run you believe is local quietly connects to the shared venue — with a 32-character ARENA_TOKEN attached, which the local venue would have ignored because it runs with AUTH_REQUIRED=false. In week 9 that is how a test rogue bot attacks the class market in front of everyone.

Its cousin: make kill is port-blind. It pkills every exchange.server on the machine, so on a shared lab machine agree who runs it.

Fix: print the URL you actually connected to, in every process, at startup — that is why the pre-flight’s first line is the URL and whether it is local. Set EXCHANGE_HOST=localhost explicitly on every client in a local run rather than relying on the absence of a variable.

Reviewing the PDF instead of the repo

A review that paraphrases someone’s abstract back at them is worth no marks and helps nobody. The reproducibility policy exists precisely so that a reviewer can re-run three commands and compare: the performance metrics at the team’s own claimed trial count, the microstructure statistics of their tape, and the surveillance rules with the known market maker exempted.

The most common finding is not fraud, it is definition drift — a different annualisation, a different data vintage, a Sharpe computed on marks rather than days. Both parties learn something, and the disagreement is the deliverable.

Fix: file the review in their repo as reviews/<your-team>.md, with a score and evidence in every rubric row, the commit SHA you reproduced from, and whether make tearsheet ran. Then the three sections that carry the marks: what is strong, what you could not verify, and the number you would not sign.

Excusing the backtest-to-live gap instead of decomposing it

“Live underperformed because of latency and market conditions” is not a Phase-2 report. The gap has four measurable parts and you have the tools for all of them: fees from the TCA report, spread from the effective-versus-quoted comparison, impact from the implementation shortfall against your decision price, and unfilled intent from the opportunity-cost term.

Whatever remains after those four is the part of the market your model does not contain — and naming it honestly is worth more than making it small.

Fix: one table, four rows plus a residual, reconciling to the difference between the backtested P&L and the live P&L. Put it on page one. If the components do not sum to the gap, that is a reconciliation failure and week 7’s rule applies: nothing after it is graded.

Part 6

6Do the work

In the lab (~75 min)

  • Pre-flight green, then the second live scored session. Keep your own order log; copy the tape path into the report before you leave.
  • Two peer reviews, filed in the reviewed teams’ repos, with a score and evidence in every rubric row and the commit SHA you reproduced from.
  • Complete the Phase-2 checklist for your track, including the track-specific row and the AI-USE note.
  • Mark every exam concept you cannot explain in two sentences; the room spends the remaining minutes on whatever is marked most.
  • Clean up: make kill, make prune KEEP=10, and delete any tape you generated.

Phase 2 · due this week (21%)

  • A repo that runs on a clean checkout, a documented data contract, and a signal specification dated in git history before the results.
  • A costed walk-forward, purged CV wherever a model is fitted, and an overfitting section with the honest configuration count and the deflated Sharpe at that count.
  • Two live sessions, a TCA + attribution report, and a surveillance check on your own tape plus one clean control.
  • The tearsheet defence follows the exam. Bring the three numbers from week 9 on the first page, because that is the first thing we will ask about.
